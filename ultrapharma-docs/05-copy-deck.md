# 05 · Copy Deck

Every string. The existing site's best writing is kept; the rest is rewritten outward, toward the client's problem rather than the firm's category names.

**As built**, this deck is directional rather than exact for pages refined after the initial build: headings, standfirsts and step copy on `/contact`, `/insights` and `/services/reimbursement` were extended with more detail during the refinement pass (see `DESIGN-SOURCE.md`). The rules in `§1` still govern every word actually shipped, including the extended copy: no unverifiable claim, no superlative, name the thing precisely. Where an early draft of the extended copy broke that rule, it was corrected rather than kept, logged in `DESIGN-SOURCE.md` rather than here.

## 1. Voice

The reader is a regulatory affairs director, a market access lead, or a founder at a device company. They are technically literate, sceptical by profession, and scanning for evidence that you have done this before.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Registered is not the same as reimbursed." | "Unlock seamless market access." |
| "BPTAP was gazetted on 23 April 2025 and inaugurated in May." | "We stay ahead of the regulatory curve." |
| "A cost-effectiveness analysis with a local comparator." | "Data-driven insights for smarter decisions." |
| "Fewer questions from regulators, shorter review cycles." | "Best-in-class submission excellence." |

### Rules

1. **Sentence case** except `--t-label`.
2. **Name the thing.** PPB, SHA, BPTAP, eCTD, CTD Module 3, EAC. Precision is the product; vagueness reads as inexperience.
3. **Every claim is checkable or it is cut.** `01 D-03`.
4. **Dates and reference numbers in mono**, because they are checkable facts and the typography should say so.
5. **No superlatives.** No leading, world-class, best-in-class, cutting-edge, seamless, unlock, empower, journey, revolutionise, robust.
6. **No exclamation marks. No em dashes. No emoji.**
7. **State the consequence, not the activity.** "Pre-submission audits" is an activity. "Fewer questions from regulators" is why anyone cares.
8. **Second person for the client, first person plural for the firm.** Sparingly on both.
9. **Never imply the firm manufactures, dispenses or prescribes.** It advises.

## 4. `/services/reimbursement`

The most important new page. Corrected against the verified record in `01 §4`.

**Page title** `Health product assessment and national benefit listing`

**Standfirst**
`Preparing the evidence that supports inclusion in Kenya's national health benefits package, so an approved product becomes a funded one.`

### 01 · What changed

`Kenya's Social Health Insurance Act 2023 replaced the National Hospital Insurance Fund with the Social Health Authority. Coverage now flows through three funds: the Primary Healthcare Fund, the Social Health Insurance Fund, and the Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund.`

`The Ministry of Health established the Benefits Package and Tariffs Advisory Panel to guide what those funds cover and at what tariff. The panel was gazetted through Gazette Notice No. 5044 of 23 April 2025 and inaugurated in May 2025, chaired by Prof. Walter Jaoko, with its secretariat at the Centre for Epidemiological Modelling and Analysis at the University of Nairobi. Its method is health technology assessment.`

Dates and the gazette number render in `--t-data`.

### 02 · What it means for your product

`Registration establishes that a product may be sold. It does not establish that anyone will pay for it.`

`A product outside the benefits package competes on out-of-pocket price against a funded alternative. In a market where most patients are covered through SHA, that is a decisive commercial disadvantage, and it is invisible on a regulatory timeline because it sits entirely after approval.`

### 03 · What we do

**HTA dossier development**
`Assembling the evidence a health technology assessment requires: clinical effectiveness against an appropriate local comparator, safety, and the value case in a form assessors can evaluate.`

**Cost-effectiveness and budget-impact modelling**
`Incremental cost-effectiveness analysis using Kenyan cost and epidemiological inputs, and a budget-impact model showing what inclusion would mean for the relevant fund over a defined horizon.`

**Stakeholder engagement and SHA liaison**
`Structured engagement with the Ministry of Health, the Social Health Authority and the panel's secretariat, sequenced so evidence arrives when it can be considered.`

### 04 · The evidence a submission needs

Rendered as `EvidenceList`. This section is the credibility proof.

| # | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 01 | Clinical effectiveness against a comparator in current Kenyan practice |
| 02 | Safety profile and adverse-event data |
| 03 | Incremental cost-effectiveness analysis with local cost inputs |
| 04 | Budget-impact model against the relevant fund |
| 05 | Local epidemiological and disease-burden data |
| 06 | Equity and access considerations |
| 07 | Implementation feasibility within existing service delivery |

### 05 · How we work

`Phase 1 · Feasibility assessment.` `We establish whether the evidence base supports a submission, and what is missing. Deliverable: a feasibility opinion and evidence gap register.`

`Phase 2 · Evidence generation.` `Closing the gaps: comparator selection, model construction, local data sourcing. Deliverable: a completed economic model and dossier draft.`

`Phase 3 · Engagement.` `Structured stakeholder engagement and submission support. Deliverable: a final dossier and an engagement record.`

`Phase 4 · Follow-through.` `Responding to questions and supporting the review process to determination.`

### The caveat, per `01 D-04`

Until the client confirms a published applicant pathway, the page carries this line under section 03, plainly and without apology:

`Kenya's HTA process is new and still taking shape. We work with the process as it currently operates, and we will tell you plainly what is established and what is not.`

That sentence does more for credibility with a sophisticated buyer than any promise would.
