The East African Community medicines regulatory harmonisation framework is often presented as though registering in one member state carries a product into the others. It reduces duplication substantially. It does not remove the second filing.

## What harmonisation genuinely removes

The framework aligns technical requirements and supports joint assessment across participating national authorities. The practical gain is that the scientific core of a dossier, the quality, non-clinical and clinical modules, is built once and travels. That is the expensive part of the file, and not rebuilding it per market is a real saving.

Joint assessment procedures also mean a single scientific review can inform several national determinations rather than each authority repeating the work independently.

## What still has to be rebuilt

Determination remains national. Each authority issues its own marketing authorisation, and each retains requirements the framework does not displace:

- Local representation and applicant status in the market
- Labelling and packaging to national requirements, including language
- Local fees, forms and administrative modules
- National pharmacovigilance obligations and commitment tracking
- Post-approval variation handling, which is not automatically synchronised across markets

The last one is the most commonly underestimated. A portfolio registered in four markets carries four variation obligations, and they drift out of alignment quickly unless they are managed as one register rather than four projects.

## Planning consequence

Treat the second market as a dossier adaptation exercise rather than a new submission, and budget for the administrative and lifecycle layer rather than the scientific one. The cost of market two is not a fraction of market one distributed evenly; it is concentrated almost entirely in the parts of the file that harmonisation deliberately left national.
